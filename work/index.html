GTM engineering

People ask what a GTM engineer actually does, so here's the honest version: I build the machinery a startup uses to find and win customers. Sales and marketing decide who we should talk to and what we should say. I build the systems that make that happen at scale, and keep them running.

I do this full time at GoodBox, where I'm a GTM Associate. GoodBox builds conversational AI voice and chat agents for sales, support, and recruitment.

The job carries the sales side as well as the systems. I still build the machinery, and I also work the motion it feeds, which means I'm the first person to find out when something I built doesn't survive contact with a real conversation.

Day to day

What I build

Outbound research systems

Sourcing the right companies, enriching them from public evidence, and scoring every account against an ICP. Most of my week lives here.

Evaluation and reporting

An evaluation loop for GTM content — golden sets and rubric-based LLM judging — so messaging changes get decided on measured output rather than opinion. Plus the CRM data operations and funnel reporting underneath it.

Agentic workflows and internal tools

Wherever a GTM task repeats, I make software do it. Agentic workflows against production APIs, and internal tools so the team spends its time on conversations instead of busywork.

The sales motion itself

Part of my week is the selling, not the tooling: working the accounts my own systems surfaced, qualifying them in conversation, and carrying them forward. It's the shortest feedback loop I have. What breaks in a live conversation gets fixed in the system the same week.
